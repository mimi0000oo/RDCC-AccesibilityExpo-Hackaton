Interviewer: Thanks for joining today. Could you start by walking me through your background and what drew you to this role? We have about forty-five minutes, so feel free to take your time with the answer.

Candidate: Sure, happy to. I spent the last three years working on accessibility tooling for a mid-sized SaaS company, mostly focused on making data-heavy dashboards usable with screen readers and keyboard-only navigation. Before that I was on a general front-end team, but accessibility kept coming up as the thing nobody owned, so I ended up specializing in it.

Interviewer: That's a great segue into my next question. Can you describe a specific accessibility problem you solved that you're especially proud of, and walk me through how you diagnosed it, what options you considered, and why you picked the approach you shipped?

Candidate: One that stands out was a live-updating notifications panel that screen reader users couldn't reliably track, because new items were inserted without any ARIA live region, so updates were silent. I set up an `aria-live="polite"` region, throttled the announcements so it didn't become a firehose of noise, and added a visible focus ring that matched what the reader would hear, so sighted and non-sighted users got a consistent sense of "something just changed here."

Interviewer: Nice. Last one for this section — how do you usually test accessibility work before it ships? Automated tools only, manual pass, or something in between?

Candidate: Always a mix. Automated linting catches the obvious stuff — missing labels, contrast ratios, that kind of thing — but it misses anything about actual usability, so I always do a manual keyboard-only pass and at least a partial screen-reader pass with VoiceOver or NVDA before calling something done.
